### Abstract

3D reconstruction in field environments remains a long-standing challenge in computer vision due to constrained viewpoints, variable image quality, and complex scene geometry, coupled with the high accuracy requirements of downstream applications. Recent advances in stereo matching and feed-forward reconstruction networks have substantially improved reconstruction quality in general settings. However, their generalization to real-world field conditions remains limited, as commonly used benchmark datasets often fail to capture the diversity and complexity of field environments. With the emergence of agentic AI as a powerful tool across research domains, we investigate its potential to address this last-mile challenge. Given identical image sequences captured in field environments, we find that an AI coding agent achieves substantially higher reconstruction quality in significantly less time than human researchers, who often struggle to effectively adapt state-of-the-art methods to unfamiliar data. Further analysis of this human-agent performance gap unveils that agentic AI excels at identifying poorly constrained geometric features in field imagery and systematically proposing, testing, and selecting engineering strategies from a broad range of candidates to address these deficiencies. We characterize such tasks, which require continuous monitoring of image features throughout the research loop, iterative experimentation, and substantial engineering effort, as agentic-AI-shaped problems in field 3D reconstruction, where human researchers face limitations in engineering bandwidth. In the era of agentic AI, we argue that the researcher's role is shifting from pipeline implementation toward problem formulation, rigorous evaluation, scientific judgment, and the development of AI-enabling infrastructure, such as field imaging systems.
